An integer modular form of weight \(k \in \mathbb {N}\) is a sequence \(a : \mathbb {N} → \mathbb {Z}\) such that \(\sum _{n=0}^{\infty } a(n) q^n\) is a modular form of weight \(k\), where \(q = e ^{2 \pi i z}.\)

  • IntegerModularForm.Eis

For \(k \ge 2 \in \mathbb {N}\), the Eisenstein series \(E_k\) is an integer modular form of weight \(2k\) defined by

\[ E_k = 1 - \frac{2k}{B_{2k}} \sum _{n = 1}^{\infty } \sigma _{k - 1} (n) q ^n \]

where \(B_k\) is the kth Bernoulli number and \(\sigma _{k} (n)\) is the sum of the kth powers of the divisors of \(n\).

A modular form mod \(\ell \) of weight \(k \in \mathbb {Z} / (\ell - 1) \mathbb {Z}\) is a sequence \(a : \mathbb {N} → \mathbb {Z} / \ell \mathbb {Z}\) such that there exists an integer modular form \(b\) of weight \(k'\) where \(b \equiv a \mod \ell \) and \(k' \equiv k \mod (\ell - 1)\).

Definition 10 multiplication and exponentiation
✓
#

Uses

  • Definition 5

Lean declarations

  • ModularFormMod.mul
  • ModularFormMod.pow

It’s worth stating how multiplication and exponentiation are defined here, because they are not defined in the normal way. The multiplication of two modular forms mod \(\ell \) called \(a\) and \(b\) is defined as

\[ (a \cdot b) n = \sum _{x + y = n} a(x) b(y). \]

The exponentiation of a modular form mod \(\ell \) called \(a\) to the power of \(j \in \mathbb {N}\) is defined as

\[ (a^j) n = \sum _{x_1 + ... + x_j = n} \prod _{i = 1}^j a (x_i). \]

  • IntegerModularForm.G

For \( k \ne 1\) and \(c {\lt} \) dim\((k)\), write \(k\) uniquely as \(k = 6\ell + k'\), where \(k' \in \{ 0, 2, 3, 4, 5, 7\} \). \(G_{k,\, c}\) is a modular form of weight \(2k\) defined as

\[ G_{k,\, c} = E_2 ^a E_3 ^b E_2 ^{\ell - 3c} \Delta ^c \]

where \(a, b\) are the unique natural numbers with \(2a + 3b = k'.\)
We have that the set of all such \(G_{k,\, c}\) for fixed \(k\) form a basis for the modular forms of weight \(2k\).

Let \(\alpha \) be a commutative semiring, and let \(f, g : \mathbb {N} \to \alpha [[X]]\) be two sequences of power series with coefficients in \(\alpha \). We say that \(f\) and \(g\) are eventually equal, and write \(f \longrightarrow g\), if \(\forall n \in \mathbb {N}, \: \exists m \in \mathbb {N}, \: \forall \: k \le n, \forall \: j \ge m\), the kth coefficient of \(f(j)\) is equal to the kth coefficient of \(g(j)\). This is an equivalence relation.
